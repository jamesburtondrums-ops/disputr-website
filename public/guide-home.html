Energy, utilities and household services

Bills, readings, appointments and correspondence are often the key to showing what went wrong and how long the problem has continued.

Energy complaints

For an energy supply problem, identify whether the issue is with your supplier or the network operator. Common complaints include billing errors, meter problems, disputed readings, account credit, switching, supply issues and poor customer service.

Evidence to collect

  • Bills and account statements
  • Dated meter readings and photographs
  • Tariff or contract information
  • Engineer or appointment records
  • Emails, chats and complaint references
  • Evidence of costs or disruption caused by the problem

Make the formal complaint

Tell the supplier or network operator what the problem is, when it began, what you have already done and what would put it right. Keep the date you first raised the complaint.

Energy suppliers and relevant network operators must try to resolve reported complaint problems within 8 weeks. If it remains unresolved after that, or you receive a deadlock letter earlier, the complaint may be taken to the Energy Ombudsman.

What can the Energy Ombudsman do?

For complaints it can consider, the Ombudsman can require actions such as fixing the problem, explaining what happened or paying compensation. Ofgem is the regulator and does not investigate individual consumer disputes in place of the Ombudsman.

Other household servicesWater, building work, tradespeople and other home services can follow different complaint and ADR routes. Do not assume the Energy Ombudsman covers them.
