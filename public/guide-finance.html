Finance complaints

Banking, credit and financial services

A good financial complaint connects the product, what the firm did or failed to do, the effect on you, the evidence and the remedy you want.

Does this guide apply?

Use this route for complaints involving banks, lenders, credit cards, loans, overdrafts, motor finance, payment services and many other regulated financial products. Some products and complaint types have special rules, so identify the exact firm and product first.

Common issues

  • Affordability or irresponsible lending concerns
  • Fees, interest or disputed charges
  • Fraud, scams and payment disputes
  • Account restrictions or poor complaint handling
  • Credit, loan or finance agreement disputes

What evidence should you collect?

  • Statements covering the relevant period
  • The agreement, application or product terms
  • Complaint emails, letters and chat transcripts
  • Reference numbers and dates of telephone calls
  • Evidence of financial loss or other impact
  • The firm's final response, once issued

Build a chronology rather than sending a pile of unexplained documents. Note what each important document proves.

How to make the complaint

Tell the firm clearly that you are making a complaint. Identify the account or agreement, explain the events in date order, say why you believe the firm should put matters right and state the outcome you want. Keep a copy and record the date the firm received it.

For most financial complaints a firm has up to 8 weeks to consider the complaint. Some payment-service, fraud and scam complaints can have shorter response periods, so check the current rule for your complaint type.

What happens after the final response?

The final response should explain the firm's decision and how you can take the complaint further. For complaints within the Financial Ombudsman Service's jurisdiction, the usual deadline is 6 months from the date of the final response. Do not assume every financial dispute follows exactly the same deadline or eligibility rules.

ImportantKeep the final-response letter and the date on it. It can determine your escalation deadline.

If the firm rejects the complaint

Compare the final response with the points you actually raised. Note anything the firm did not answer, factual errors, missing evidence or conclusions you disagree with. If eligible, you can then present the complaint and evidence to the Financial Ombudsman Service. The Ombudsman is separate from the financial business.
