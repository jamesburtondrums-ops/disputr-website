Telecoms complaints

Mobile, broadband and phone services

Billing, service faults and contract disputes are easier to escalate when you can show exactly when the problem started and what the provider promised to do.

Common telecoms complaints

  • Incorrect bills or unexpected charges
  • Broadband, mobile or landline faults
  • Poor handling of a switch or cancellation
  • Contract or price-change disputes
  • Mis-selling or unclear contract information

Evidence to collect

  • Bills and contract documents
  • Order, installation and cancellation dates
  • Fault references and outage history
  • Relevant speed-test or service evidence
  • Emails, chats and notes of calls
  • Any deadlock letter from the provider

Make a formal complaint

Contact the provider first and, if ordinary customer service does not resolve the problem, make a formal complaint under its complaints code. Explain the problem, the steps already taken and the remedy you want.

For complaints raised from 8 April 2026, residential and qualifying small-business customers can generally access ADR once the complaint has remained unresolved for 6 weeks, or earlier if the provider issues a deadlock letter.

Which independent scheme?

Telecoms providers must belong to an approved ADR scheme. The two current schemes are CISAS and Communications Ombudsman. Your provider should identify which one applies. Ofcom regulates the sector but does not resolve individual billing or service disputes itself.

Useful checkpointIf the provider says it can do nothing more, ask whether it will issue a deadlock letter rather than simply leaving the complaint open.
