Travel complaints

Flights, delays, cancellations and bookings

Travel complaints can involve different businesses and schemes, so first identify who supplied the service that actually went wrong.

Start with the right business

Work out whether the complaint is against an airline, airport, rail operator, travel agent, booking platform, hotel or package organiser. A booking can involve several companies with different responsibilities.

Evidence to collect

  • Booking confirmation and reference numbers
  • Scheduled and actual departure or arrival times
  • Cancellation or delay messages
  • Boarding passes and tickets
  • Receipts for reasonable additional costs
  • Photos and correspondence where relevant
  • The response to your original complaint

Airline and airport complaints

Complain to the airline or airport first and give it the opportunity to respond. If you are unhappy with the response, or have not heard back within 8 weeks, an independent route may be available.

For aviation complaints, check whether the business belongs to an approved ADR scheme. The current CAA information identifies AviationADR and CEDR as aviation ADR schemes. Where an airline or airport is not in an ADR scheme, the CAA's Passenger Advice and Complaints Team may be able to consider the complaint, although PACT cannot impose a decision on an airline in the way an approved ADR body can.

Do not assume every travel complaint is an airline complaint

Rail, package holidays, accommodation and booking-platform disputes can use different rules and escalation bodies. Check the provider's complaint terms and the type of booking before relying on an aviation deadline or remedy.

Keep your costs evidencedIf you are claiming reimbursement, keep itemised receipts and explain why each additional cost resulted from the disruption.
