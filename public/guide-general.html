General complaints

Build a complaint that is easy to investigate

A strong complaint is not necessarily a long one. It makes the problem, evidence and requested outcome easy for somebody else to understand.

1. Identify the right organisation

Work out who supplied the product or service and who has the power to put the problem right. If several companies are involved, record what each one did rather than assuming they have the same responsibility.

2. Build the evidence

  • Contracts, receipts, invoices and account records
  • Emails, letters, chats and complaint references
  • Photographs or screenshots
  • A dated chronology of important events
  • Evidence of costs or losses

Keep originals where possible and use copies when sending evidence.

3. Write the complaint

Explain what happened in date order. Separate facts from assumptions. Say what you believe went wrong, refer to the evidence that supports it, explain the effect on you and state a realistic outcome you want.

4. Record the complaint date

Keep proof of when the complaint was submitted. Some sectors have formal response periods and escalation deadlines, and the date of the original complaint or final response can matter.

5. If it is rejected

Read the response against your original points. Identify unanswered issues and factual disagreements. Check whether the organisation belongs to an Ombudsman or ADR scheme and whether there is a deadline. Regulators, ADR schemes, Ombudsmen and courts perform different roles, so do not assume reporting something to a regulator resolves your individual dispute.

Keep it focusedDates + facts + evidence + impact + requested outcome is usually more useful than an angry or heavily legalistic complaint.
