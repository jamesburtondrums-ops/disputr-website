Retail complaints

Goods, services, deliveries and refunds

Be precise about what you bought, what is wrong with it and what remedy you are asking the seller to provide.

What kind of problem is it?

  • Faulty or misdescribed goods
  • A service not carried out with reasonable care
  • Missing, damaged or incorrect deliveries
  • Refund or cancellation disputes
  • Unfair or unclear contract terms
  • Digital content that does not work as expected

Evidence to collect

  • Receipt, invoice or other proof of purchase
  • Order confirmation and product description
  • Photographs or video of the fault
  • Delivery tracking and packaging evidence
  • Messages with the retailer
  • Any inspection, repair or return records

Your consumer rights matter

UK consumer law protects purchases of goods, services and digital content. For faulty goods, the Consumer Rights Act provides a short-term right to reject in qualifying circumstances within 30 days; other remedies such as repair, replacement, price reduction or refund can apply depending on the circumstances and timing.

A simple change of mind is different from a fault. Your rights can also differ depending on whether the purchase was made in a shop or at a distance, such as online.

Make the complaint useful

Tell the retailer the purchase date, order number, fault or service failure, when you discovered it and the remedy you want. Avoid vague wording such as “this is unacceptable” without explaining the actual problem.

If the retailer does not resolve it, possible next routes can include an applicable ADR scheme, card/payment protections, consumer advice or ultimately a court claim. Which route is appropriate depends on the purchase and payment method.

Proof of purchaseA traditional paper receipt is not the only possible proof. Keep card statements, emails and order confirmations too.
