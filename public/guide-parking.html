Parking

Parking charges, challenges and appeals

The first question is who issued the notice. Council and private parking cases do not follow the same appeal route.

Identify the notice

Check whether it came from a council or other public authority, a private parking company, or another issuer. Read the notice itself carefully before responding because it sets out the challenge route and applicable dates.

Evidence to collect

  • The complete parking notice and envelope if relevant
  • Photographs of signs, markings and the location
  • Payment or permit evidence
  • Entry, exit or ticket times
  • Receipts or proof of being a legitimate customer where relevant
  • Every challenge and rejection letter

Private parking

Challenge the notice with the private operator using the process on the notice. If rejected, the independent appeal service depends on the operator's accredited trade association: POPLA is used for British Parking Association members and the IAS for International Parking Community members.

Council and public-authority tickets

These use statutory challenge and tribunal procedures rather than POPLA or IAS. A notice of rejection should explain the next appeal step and deadline. For many penalty charge notices, a notice of rejection gives 28 days to pay or appeal to the independent tribunal.

Do not miss the notice deadlineParking procedures can move quickly and later charges can increase. Record every deadline exactly as shown on the notice.
